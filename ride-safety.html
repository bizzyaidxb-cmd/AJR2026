Ride Safety

Safety rides with us.

सुरक्षित सवारी, गर्व की सवारी

Every Amar Jawan Ride is a planned, marshalled, low-speed ride through city streets. Here's how we keep it safe — and what we need from you.

Safety first, always

The Amar Jawan Ride is a fundraising ride, not a race. Every ride is organised with a ride leader, trained marshals and support on the road, and moves at a slow, steady pace through the heart of the city. Safety is a shared responsibility — we plan and marshal the ride; you ride prepared, sober and within the rules below.

Safety team: safety@amarjawanride.in  ·  Helpline: +91 00000 00000  ·  Report an incident

How an AJR ride works

You're never riding alone.

Every ride is run by a team so the convoy stays safe, together and on route.

Ride Leader

Sets the pace at the front. Never ride ahead of the leader.

Trained Marshals

Positioned through the convoy to guide it and keep it together.

Corner Markers

Hold key junctions so the group stays safe and on route.

First Aid / Ambulance

Before you ride

What's mandatory.

No documents, no ride. Bring these, every time.

A helmet — for you and your pillion. No helmet, no ride.

A valid driving licence for the motorcycle you're riding.

A roadworthy bike with valid Registration (RC), insurance and PUC (pollution) certificate.

Your own insurance in order — AJR does not provide rider or accident cover (see below).

On the ride

The rules.

Helmet on, always. Non-negotiable, for rider and pillion.

Obey every traffic law — signals, lane discipline, no jumping red lights. The convoy stops for the city; the city doesn't stop for us.

No stunts. No burnouts, wheelies, bike-surfing or reckless riding. We ride to honour our soldiers — ride like it.

Never drink and ride. Zero tolerance. Anyone riding under the influence is removed from the ride and reported to the authorities.

One pillion per bike. No tripling — ever.

Stay with the group. If you're separated, don't speed to catch up — the ride has regroup points.

Pillion & family

Bring your people.

One pillion is welcome on every bike (never more than one), and families are part of what we ride for — so we're glad to have them along. Your pillion must wear a helmet too, and for young children we ask you to ride extra cautiously and use your best judgment.

On ride day

You're covered on the road.

Marshals ride throughout the convoy, first-aid and ambulance support is on standby, and a support vehicle sweeps the rear to recover any bike that breaks down — dropping it somewhere safe or at the finish, where it's yours to collect. Make sure your bike is in good shape before the ride so you're not the one being swept up.

Medical & insurance — please read

The Amar Jawan Ride does not provide any rider insurance or accident cover. Every rider takes part at their own risk and is responsible for arranging their own valid motorcycle and personal insurance. By registering, you accept the ride waiver and confirm your documents and cover are in order.

Weather & changes

Safety before schedule.

If the weather turns unsafe — or local authorities require it — a city's ride may be postponed or changed at short notice. Any change is announced on your city's official WhatsApp group, so keep notifications on after you register.

Safety FAQ

Your questions, answered.

What does AJR do to prevent collisions?

Every ride is slow, led and marshalled, with a mandatory safety briefing and regroup points along the route. We also remind all registered riders of the safety rules before ride day.

What if my bike breaks down?

A support vehicle sweeps the rear of the convoy and will recover your bike to a safe spot or the finish, where it's yours to collect. Please make sure your bike is roadworthy before you ride.

What documents do I need to ride?

A valid driving licence, plus your bike's Registration (RC), insurance and PUC (pollution) certificate. Carry them on the day.

Can I bring a pillion or my family?

Yes — one pillion per bike (no tripling). Families are welcome; your pillion must wear a helmet too, and for young children please ride extra cautiously.

Is a helmet really mandatory?

Yes — for the rider and the pillion, with no exceptions. No helmet, no ride.

What if the weather is bad?

The ride may be postponed for safety, or on the instruction of local authorities. Any change is announced on your city's WhatsApp group — keep notifications on.

What if the ride is very large?

Marshals manage the group, capacity is monitored per city, and the safety briefing uses a loud-hailer where needed so everyone hears the key points.

How do I handle the heat?

Indian ride days can be hot. Hydrate before and after, carry water, and dress for the weather under your gear. If you feel unwell, tell a marshal.

What if I get separated from the group?

Don't speed to catch up. Ride safely to the next regroup point or to the finish; marshals are there to help.

How is speeding controlled?

The pace is set by the ride leader and held by the marshals. By design, it's a slow, low-speed ride through busy city streets.

What about riding under the influence?

Zero tolerance. Anyone found intoxicated is removed from the ride and reported to the authorities.

Am I insured on the ride?

No. AJR provides no insurance or accident cover. You ride at your own risk and must arrange your own valid motorcycle and personal insurance.

A car cut into the convoy or caused an incident — what do I do?

Prioritise everyone's safety and don't confront the driver. Note the vehicle details (make, model, colour, number plate) and pass them to the marshals or police. If someone is hurt, attend to them first and call for medical help immediately.

What if the police stop or cancel the ride?

We comply, always. Rider safety and the law come first, and any change is communicated through your city's channels.
