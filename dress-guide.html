Ride Day Style

Dress Proudly Indian.

एक सवारी, हर रंग

One ride, every colour of India. However you wear your pride — simple or extravagant — bring it. Let's make this the most colourful convoy the country has ever seen.

The theme

Come as your most proudly Indian self.

The Amar Jawan Ride is a themed ride, and the theme is simple. There's no dress code — only a spirit. From a small flag pinned to a plain shirt to a fully blinged-out sherwani, every expression of India's colour, diversity and unity belongs on this ride. Go as simple or as extravagant as you like — just bring the colour.

Keep It Simple

Not into dressing up? A crisp shirt with a small Indian flag or a flame pin is all it takes. Everyone belongs on this ride.

Bring the Tradition

Kurta-pyjama, Pathani, a bandhgala or Jodhpuri jacket, a sherwani — timeless Indian menswear in handloom fabrics never misses.

Go All Out

This is the day to shine. Brocade, mirror-work, zardozi, bold embroidery — the more blinged-out, the better.

For the Women Who Ride

A saree draped to ride, a salwar-kameez, a lehenga-inspired look, or your own sharp bandhgala — make a statement and own the road.

Ride in Colour

Bandhani, ikat, phulkari, kalamkari, block prints — wear India's textiles loud and proud. Mix regions, mix colours.

Crown It

A safa or pagri, a Mysore peta, a Gandhi or Himachali cap, a bright dupatta — headwear for the meet and the photos (helmet goes on for the ride).

Rep Your Region

Fly your state's colours — Kerala's off-white-and-gold, Rajasthan's mirror-work, Punjab's phulkari, the Northeast's weaves. India in one convoy.

The Finishing Touches

Juttis or mojris (that still work the gears!), a stole or angavastram, brooches, bangles, a bright pocket square. The details make it yours.

Fusion on Wheels

Indian meets the road: a Nehru jacket over a riding tee, an angavastram over your jacket. Your rules.

Carry the Salute

Add a quiet tribute — a flame pin, a ribbon, or the AJR badge — so the reason we ride rides with you.

Two things to remember

Style is up to you. These aren't.

Helmet On, Always

A helmet is mandatory — no exceptions. It's a low-speed, planned ride within city limits, but the helmet stays on. Style is optional; this isn't.

Honour, Don't Imitate

Out of respect and by Indian law, no military uniforms, camouflage, insignia or medals of any kind. We honour our soldiers through colour and spirit — never by wearing what they earned.

Come as your proudest self.

Plan your look, bring the colour, and ride for the families of the brave.
