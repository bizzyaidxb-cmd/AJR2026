Our Story

We ride so they're never ridden out of memory.

अमर जवान, अमर रहें

The Amar Jawan Ride is a nationwide movement of motorcyclists raising funds for the families of the soldiers who gave everything for India.

The beginning

It began on a road to Ladakh.

In 2008, two brothers — Prateek and Sarthak — took their first long ride together into Ladakh. They'd been crossing India on their motorcycles for years, meeting thousands of riders along the way, but this road was different. When the weather turned and left them stranded, it was the Indian armed forces who brought them back to safety.

In those hours among the soldiers, the brothers felt something they've never forgotten — the quiet strength, the hospitality, and the stories: of men who guard our borders through every night, and of the ones who never came home. The mountains gave them their lives back. They rode away owing a debt.

The question

And the families they left behind?

One question stayed with them long after the ride ended: when a soldier gives their life for the country, what happens to the family they leave behind?

Searching for the answer, the brothers found people already devoted to it — organisations that have worked for years for the widows, children and parents of India's martyrs. What they lacked wasn't commitment. It was a steady, dependable flow of funds to keep going, year after year. That gap became the reason for the ride.

How it works

100% goes to the families' cause.

The Amar Jawan Ride brings riders together in cities across India — each region riding on its own day — for one shared cause. We don't reinvent the work already being done. Every single rupee raised is passed on to a network of qualified NGOs with proven track records in serving martyrs' families — not a paisa less.

We're finalising the partner organisations we'll ride for, each chosen for years of dedicated, accountable service.

The founders

Two brothers. One promise.

What started as a debt owed on a mountain road became a movement. Prateek Darolia and Sarthak Darolia built the Amar Jawan Ride so that every rider in India could carry the same salute they first felt in Ladakh — and turn it into something the families of our soldiers can count on.
